Why I record videos in chunks

Drew Williams

For a long time I tried to record videos in one take. I'd hit record, try to say everything perfectly, mess up halfway through, and start over. It was slow and it made me sound stiff.

Now I record in chunks. Each chunk is one idea, and I splice them together afterwards.

One idea at a time

When a chunk only has to carry one idea, I can actually focus on it. I don't have to remember where I'm going next or how I'll get there. I just say the thing as clearly as I can.

If a chunk goes badly, I only redo that chunk. Not the whole video.

Talking points, not a script

I don't write out full sentences anymore. A few bullet points per idea is enough:

  • What the idea is
  • Why it matters
  • One example
  • Where it leads next

Reading a script word for word sounds like reading. Talking from bullet points sounds like talking.

The edit does the heavy lifting

Splicing chunks together means the pacing is decided in the edit, not while I'm nervous in front of the camera. I can reorder ideas, cut the weak ones, and tighten everything up.

Start small

If you want to try it, take your next video and split it into three ideas. Record each one on its own. You'll be surprised how much easier it gets.
